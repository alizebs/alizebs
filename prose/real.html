Are We Real?


"I think, therefore I am,” a popular quote by French philosopher René Descartes, emerges from the concept of solipsism — a theory that suggests that your own mind is the only thing that can be known for certain to exist, and everything and everyone else are possibly only a figment of your imagination. This might sound absurd, but there is no way to disprove it.



Many theories and beliefs imply that reality is an illusion. In 2003, philosopher Nick Bostrom proposed the theory of simulation hypothesis, which claims we are living in a computer simulation created by superior beings.
Multiple ancient ideologies suggest we are reborn as another person or even an animal after we die, implying that you and I have lived as someone else; we just don't remember.
The Hindu mythology of maya states that the material world is an illusion, and the true reality is the unchanging spiritual essence. Abrahamic beliefs, on the other hand, put forward that this reality is a test for the next.
While science relies on observable reality and treats it as certain, many philosophies challenge this assumption. What do you think — are we real?
