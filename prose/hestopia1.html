Chapter 1


The Original Sin



A hand reached a blue apple, hanging on a branch of a tremendous tree. The ends of its branches, each and every one, were dug into the ground. And on the ground was a thin layer of water, with black and gray powder almost dissolving in it, at unorganized distances. The trembling fingers brushed the blue skin of the apple as soft and silent wind blew through the water producing a low murmuring of the wise water.


“Humanity was then condemned to live in a hell of prejudice and hatred called earth,” cherubs with draped cloths were carved onto the marble of the high walls, “that was then, when Eve, in the garden of Eden, committed the original sin,” the priest emphasized, standing on an elevated floor of wood, in front of diversely aged people sitting solemnly on wooden benches as misty sunlight from the tinted windows shone on their fair faces. The priest continued in the same resonant, loud voice, “The sin wasn’t envy, it wasn’t pride, it wasn’t greed, neither was it lust, nor was it wrath,” he spread his arms and lifted his face, his cross pendant swung in the thick air, “The original sin — was curiosity.”
Under the umbrella of an outdoor restaurant table, Gesha said to Henry with a scoff as she sipped her orange juice off a straw,  “Imagine telling a FARS scientist that curiosity is a sin, that's why I don't believe in religion.”
“No, the sermon the priest gave wasn’t biblical. God never forbade curiosity, nor would he ever. The original sin wasn’t curiosity — it was disobedience to a higher being.”  Henry interrupted, as he took a bite off his apple.
“Yes to whatever you say, now pick your apple and get up, we have to inspect the particle observed by our M16T this morning.” said Gesha indifferently, picking her car keys and juice cup off the table.

A sand-colored flat cuboid flashed with short lines of white light, arranged differently every flash, under the shadow of dark, smoky air. The air dimmed the whole atmosphere, as an eclipse dims the sun. Tall figures of black, loose, and flowing of what appeared to be a heavy cloth wrapped into a cylindrical shape, like a very tall human body in a thick thobe; a similar cloth, smaller in length by at least ten times was in the center, just above the larger one, like a head draped in a thick cloth, covered completely.
Hundreds of thousands of these figures moved in every direction in an unorganized manner. Tall structures of sandstone, reaching the sky, far across the horizon. Above the sky, was a circular division that appeared to bend the stars and the galaxies around it, and a completely different night sky was visible through it. Near the land, which was smooth, slightly dusty, and maroon in color, multiple obscure, orbiting structures of blue sandstone and metal were hung in the sky, each shooting a dark blue beam of laser into the hole which bent the light around its circumference.
Beneath it, a huge flag carved on a flat block of gaseous matter hung between two of the tall sandstone structures, larger in width than three or four of the sandstone skyscrapers were tall. 
Way down, a giant millipede with its glorious legs, moved across, and then from the middle of its body, it instantaneously turned into a dense gas of the same colors as its body that moved at a higher speed through the air. Many of the tall black figures did the same, though with their ‘head’ covered in the gas. Hundreds of them moved through the tall trees whose branches bore strange fruits, and the end of every branch was covered with a small pit of soil. 
Enormous numbers of these trees stretched beyond the horizon in thin strips spread across the land in organized routes. Under these trees, machines with thin dark blue panels and a grumpy visible engine of unseen material pumped, in small coughs — as if running out of power, clear, slightly white air that pushed the dark air away. 
In a vast expanse of ground, all the tree routes from all sides diverged away. A great seething, swarming throng of millions of the black creatures was scattered across it, all moving their own ways. Hundreds of small ships of metal, sandstone and gaseous matters flew with loud rumbles here and there. On each end of the ground were two sprawling vertical circular megastructures of metal that rotated at a very slow pace. On them were thousands of windows and lights, irregularly arranged, barely visible through the gray fog.
In the center of each of them, was a grand vault of incredibly hard metal. And behind many layers of such metal, sandstones of obscure colors, laser rays and a final layer of thick gaseous matter was a minuscule glass capsule filled with pure water held by four metal rods on each corner, spinning at a blistering pace.
Behind one of the many thousands of windows on the left wheel, currently at the topmost compartment, was a large glass box filled with pale liquid of a color beyond the visible light. Multiple of the small ships were flying arround this and adjascent compartments. In the liquid, a ten-year-old girl was floating in the liquid, unconscious. She was wearing a brown, faded frock with a pretty floral pattern and dull spencer jacket tied by a flowing ribbon around her torso. Her eyes were half-open, blank and featureless. Wires were connected to her lower eyelids, her forehead, her temples, her wrist veins, her flanks, and thicker wires behind her neck and lower head; all stretching to the top, closed end of the specimen vat.
